Why this exists

The status quo ages faster than it ships

01
Standard courses age in weeks, not years
AI platforms release significant updates every few weeks. A course finalized in Q1 has stale screenshots by Q2 graduation.
02
Human SMEs can't monitor at machine speed
Tracking release notes, scoring significance, and drafting content within a 14-day sprint requires always-on vigilance no human team can sustain.
03
Full course rebuilds are slow and wasteful
When one platform updates a feature, rebuilding a 4-hour course to reflect it is a multi-week effort. Learners see outdated content in the meantime.
04
Returning learners have no clear re-entry path
A learner who finished the core 8 weeks ago has no efficient way to get current without replaying content they've already mastered.
The AI-first response
An AI research agent monitors changelogs, release notes, and blogs 24/7, flagging instructionally significant changes automatically.
The agent drafts the full update module ready for SME review. Humans start from a finished first draft, not a blank page.
Every AI decision, human edit, and approval is logged immutably. Every published drop has full traceable lineage.
SMEs approve or reject with one click. Approved content packages to SCORM and publishes automatically. No re-authoring.
Catch-up modules are AI-compiled, reviewed by a human editor, then published. Returning learners get current in under an hour.
Content Architecture

Three layers. One living curriculum.

Each layer operates on its own cadence, versioned independently, reusable across the catalog, without constant full rebuilds.

Foundational Core Course

The anchor layer. For working professionals with zero prior AI experience. Covers concepts and frameworks that evolve slowly relative to the tools. Reviewed quarterly, updated only when meaningful conceptual shifts occur.

A learner who completes this course has the vocabulary to learn any AI platform. This stability is intentional.

Bi-Weekly Update Drops + Catch-Up

The heartbeat of the living curriculum. Every two weeks a 15–30 min module publishes covering one AI development of practical significance. AI-selected, SME-approved, live-validated before publish.

Each drop is modular by design: surfaced standalone, appended to its parent platform course, or compiled into a catch-up bundle. No re-authoring needed for any surface.

Drop length15–30 min per drop
Drop cadenceEvery 2 weeks, year-round (26/yr)

Production Governance

SME roles, sprint protocol & backup

Two distinct SME tracks. A documented 14-day sprint. A named backup for every platform. The bi-weekly cadence holds regardless of personnel changes.

Core SME
Foundational Course & Cross-Layer Consistency
  • Deep expertise in AI concepts, pedagogy, and adult learning design
  • Owns the Foundational Core Course and its quarterly review cycle
  • Reviews all platform courses and update drops for instructional consistency, preventing gaps, redundancies, and contradictions across layers
  • Decides when industry movement warrants a new foundational learning objective or a v2.0 version bump
  • Not required to be a hands-on practitioner of individual tools; required to understand how those tools fit the broader AI landscape
  • Proposed Core SMEs subject to Chegg Skills approval before production begins
Platform SME
Per-Tool Practitioner & Live Validation Owner
  • Active, hands-on practitioner for each AI platform covered. Current, demonstrable experience required. Academic-only profiles not accepted.
  • One named Primary SME and one named Backup SME per platform at contract start
  • Owns the Live Validation gate (step 5): tests every factual claim against the live tool at time of publish, not time of draft
  • Reviews AI-generated drafts in side-by-side interface; edits logged as diffs to the audit trail
  • Responsible for flagging when a platform release requires a full course version bump vs. a standard update drop
  • All proposed Platform SMEs subject to Chegg Skills approval; Chegg reserves the right to reject any proposed SME

14-Day Sprint Breakdown

Every update drop follows this cadence. Days are working days within the two-week window.

Days 1–2
AI
Signal Detection & Scoring
Agent crawls monitored sources. Each development scored 0–10 on applicability, recency, novelty vs prior drops, and teachability. Candidates above threshold surface automatically.
Days 3–4
Human
Topic Selection & Brief
Content manager reviews candidate queue with AI rationales. Approves or overrides top pick. Sprint brief issued to Platform SME and AI drafting pipeline.
Days 5–8
AI
Draft Generation
Agent produces complete module draft: objective, context, core instruction, practical example, key takeaway, quiz with answer keys. [VERIFY] flags uncertain claims for SME attention.
Days 9–11
Human
SME Review & Live Validation
Platform SME reviews draft inline. Approves, edits, or requests AI revision. Then runs live validation: every factual claim tested against current platform state. All actions logged immutably.
Days 12–14
Editorial
Chegg Review & Publish
Chegg editor performs second-pass review: editorial standards, WCAG 2.1 AA compliance, and house style. On approval, content packages to SCORM and publishes automatically on sprint date.

Backup Protocol

The bi-weekly cadence is a contract. Personnel unavailability is not an acceptable reason for a missed sprint window.

Rule 01
Named backup for every platform
Each platform has a Primary and a Backup SME identified at contract signing. Both bios submitted to Chegg for approval before production begins.
Rule 02
48-hour handoff SLA
If Primary SME becomes unavailable, Backup SME assumes all sprint responsibilities within 48 hours. Vendor notifies Chegg content manager immediately. No silent substitutions.
Rule 03
Maintained knowledge base
Platform-specific style notes, prior drop summaries, and platform quirks are documented in a shared knowledge base. Backup SME can onboard to an active sprint within a single working day.
